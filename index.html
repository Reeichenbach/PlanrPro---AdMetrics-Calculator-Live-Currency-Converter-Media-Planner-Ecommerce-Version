🛒 E-Commerce Media Planning — What's Different

E-commerce media planning is fundamentally different from lead generation. In lead gen, you optimise for Cost Per Lead (CPL). In e-commerce, you optimise for ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) — and more importantly, your Break-even ROAS based on your margin structure.

The core e-commerce equation:
Revenue = Budget ÷ CPC × CVR × AOV
ROAS = Revenue ÷ Ad Spend
Profitable only when: ROAS ≥ Break-even ROAS (= 1 ÷ Gross Margin)

A 4x ROAS sounds great — but means nothing without knowing your margin. A 4x ROAS with 20% gross margin is loss-making. A 4x ROAS with 60% gross margin is highly profitable.

📐 The E-Commerce Media Planning Framework
  • 1
    Know Your Unit Economics FirstAOV, Gross Margin, COGS, Shipping costs, Return rate. Calculate your Break-even ROAS before spending a single rupee on ads.
  • 2
    Set Your Target ROASTarget ROAS = Break-even ROAS × 1.3–1.5 (profit buffer). Google should run 20–30% above blended. Meta at or near blended.
  • 3
    Allocate Budget by Funnel StageTop of Funnel (Awareness): 20–30% · Mid Funnel (Consideration): 20–30% · Bottom of Funnel (Purchase/Retargeting): 40–60%
  • 4
    Use Platform-Appropriate KPIsGoogle Search: ROAS + CVR + QS. Google Shopping: ROAS + CTR + conversion value. Meta: ROAS + Hook Rate + Frequency + Add-to-Cart rate.
  • 5
    Measure Blended ROAS / MERDon't trust platform-reported ROAS (double counting). Use MER = Total Revenue ÷ Total Marketing Spend as your north star metric.
  • 6
    Optimise AOV RelentlesslyEvery ₹100 increase in AOV at the same spend = ROAS improvement with zero extra cost. Bundles, upsells, free-shipping thresholds all raise AOV.
📖 E-Commerce Specific Terms
ROASReturn on Ad Spend — Revenue ÷ Ad Spend. The primary e-com efficiency metric.
AOVAverage Order Value — Total Revenue ÷ Total Orders. Higher AOV = better ROAS at same spend.
BE-ROASBreak-even ROAS — 1 ÷ Gross Margin. The minimum ROAS to not lose money on ads.
CVRPurchase Conversion Rate — % of clicks/sessions that complete a purchase. E-com avg: 2–4%.
CPACost Per Acquisition/Purchase — Budget ÷ Orders. Should be below your max allowable CPA.
MERMarketing Efficiency Ratio — Total Revenue ÷ Total Marketing Spend. The real blended ROAS.
COGSCost of Goods Sold — Direct cost of products sold. AOV − COGS = Gross Profit per order.
Gross Margin(Revenue − COGS) ÷ Revenue × 100. Your margin before ad spend. Determines break-even ROAS.
DPADynamic Product Ads (Meta) — Automatically shows users products they viewed/added to cart.
PMaxPerformance Max (Google) — AI-driven campaign across all Google channels. Median ROAS: 2.57x.
Hook Rate% of video viewers who watch first 3 seconds. Target: 25–35%+. Below 25% = creative not stopping scroll.
Add-to-Cart% of visitors who add to cart. Industry avg: 5–8%. Key mid-funnel signal for retargeting.
Cart Abandon% who add to cart but don't purchase. Industry avg: 70–75%. Retargeting these users is highest ROAS.
LTV / CLVCustomer Lifetime Value — Total revenue from a customer over time. Higher LTV = can afford higher CPA.
nCACNew Customer Acquisition Cost — Cost to acquire a net-new customer. Critical for DTC scaling.
CPMCost Per 1,000 Impressions — Meta's primary pricing model. India e-com: ₹80–₹200.
🔵 Google Ads E-Commerce Formula Chain
Start with budget → derive everything downward
Clicks = Budget ÷ CPC e.g. ₹1,00,000 ÷ ₹18 = 5,556
Impressions = Clicks ÷ CTR e.g. 5,556 ÷ 0.055 = 1,01,018
Orders = Clicks × CVR e.g. 5,556 × 0.032 = 178
Revenue = Orders × AOV e.g. 178 × ₹1,800 = ₹3,20,400
ROAS = Revenue ÷ Budget e.g. ₹3,20,400 ÷ ₹1,00,000 = 3.20x
CPA = Budget ÷ Orders e.g. ₹1,00,000 ÷ 178 = ₹562
Profitability formulas
Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ Gross Margin% e.g. 1 ÷ 0.45 = 2.22x
Max Allowable CPA = AOV × Margin% e.g. ₹1,800 × 0.45 = ₹810
Gross Profit = (Revenue × Margin%) − Budget e.g. (₹3,20,400 × 0.45) − ₹1,00,000 = ₹44,180
Net Revenue = Revenue × (1 − Return Rate) e.g. ₹3,20,400 × 0.92 = ₹2,94,768
🔴 Meta Ads E-Commerce Formula Chain
CPM-based — start with budget → impressions
Impressions = (Budget ÷ CPM) × 1,000 e.g. (₹80,000 ÷ ₹130) × 1,000 = 6,15,385
Clicks = Impressions × CTR e.g. 6,15,385 × 0.019 = 11,692
Orders = Clicks × Purchase CVR e.g. 11,692 × 0.021 = 246
Revenue = Orders × AOV e.g. 246 × ₹1,500 = ₹3,68,880
ROAS = Revenue ÷ Budget e.g. ₹3,68,880 ÷ ₹80,000 = 4.61x
CPC = CPM ÷ (CTR × 10) e.g. ₹130 ÷ (1.9 × 10) = ₹6.84
Reach = Impressions ÷ Frequency e.g. 6,15,385 ÷ 2.2 = 2,79,720
🟡 Universal E-Commerce Formulas
Unit economics & blended metrics
Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ Gross Margin%
Target ROAS = Break-even ROAS × 1.3 (30% profit buffer)
Max Allow. CPA = AOV × Gross Margin% − Shipping − (AOV × Gateway%)
Blended ROAS/MER = Total Revenue ÷ Total Marketing Spend
MER % = Total Marketing Spend ÷ Revenue × 100
Contribution Margin = AOV × Margin% − Shipping − (AOV × Gateway%) − CPA
AOV Improvement ROAS = (New AOV × Orders) ÷ Ad Spend
Cart Abandon Rate = (Add to Cart − Purchases) ÷ Add to Cart × 100
⚡ ROAS Shortcut: If your gross margin is M%, your break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ M.
45% margin → BE-ROAS = 2.22x · 30% margin → BE-ROAS = 3.33x · 60% margin → BE-ROAS = 1.67x
Always know your break-even before setting ROAS targets.

📈 E-Commerce Forecasting — The Right Approach

E-commerce forecasting is more complex than lead gen because your profitability depends on margin, AOV, return rates, and LTV — not just a CPL target. Always start with unit economics, never with channel metrics.

❌ Wrong approach: "We'll spend ₹1L and get a 4x ROAS."
No margin context. No break-even calculation. No AOV assumption. Dangerously incomplete.
✅ Right approach: "Our AOV is ₹1,800, gross margin is 45%. Break-even ROAS = 2.22x. We need target ROAS of 2.89x (30% buffer). At ₹1L budget, ₹18 CPC, 5.5% CTR, 3.2% CVR → 178 orders × ₹1,800 = ₹3.2L revenue = 3.2x ROAS ✓ Profitable."
🔵 Google Ads Worked Example — Fashion Brand

Search campaign, Delhi + Mumbai, Month 1 · Budget = ₹1,00,000 · AOV = ₹1,800 · Margin = 45%

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ 0.45 = 2.22x
Target ROAS = 2.22 × 1.30 = 2.89x (floor)
Clicks = ₹1,00,000 ÷ ₹18 (CPC) = 5,556
Impressions = 5,556 ÷ 0.055 (CTR) = 1,01,018
Orders = 5,556 × 0.032 (CVR) = 178
Revenue = 178 × ₹1,800 (AOV) = ₹3,20,400
ROAS = ₹3,20,400 ÷ ₹1,00,000 = 3.20x ✓ Profitable
CPA = ₹1,00,000 ÷ 178 = ₹562
Gross Profit= (₹3,20,400×0.45) − ₹1,00,000 = ₹44,180 profit

Key e-com sensitivity: A 10% drop in CVR hurts ROAS by 10%. A ₹100 increase in AOV improves ROAS by (₹100 × orders ÷ budget)x — often 0.1–0.3x ROAS improvement with zero extra spend.
🎯 E-Commerce Campaign Strategy Framework
  • 1
    Prospecting (Top of Funnel) — 30–40% of budgetGoogle: Broad match + PMAX · Meta: Lookalike audiences (1–5%), Interest targeting, Advantage+ Shopping. Goal: build retargeting pools profitably.
  • 2
    Retargeting (Bottom of Funnel) — 40–50% of budgetGoogle: Remarketing lists (RLSA), Dynamic Remarketing · Meta: Dynamic Product Ads (DPA) — product viewers, add-to-cart abandoners, checkout abandoners. Highest ROAS in the funnel (3–6x).
  • 3
    Branded + Competitor (10–20% of budget)Google: Brand keywords (lowest CPC, highest CVR) · Competitor brand terms. Never ignore branded — it's the highest ROAS campaign you'll run.
  • 4
    Seasonal Budget ShiftingIndia e-com peaks: Diwali (Oct), Dussehra, Big Billion Days, Republic Day, Women's Day. Increase budgets 2–3x. Reduce ROAS targets 10–15% to allow volume growth.
  • 5
    Creative Strategy for MetaHook rate is king — first 3 seconds determine everything. UGC testimonials drive 2.4x higher CVR than product-only ads. Rotate creatives every 2–3 weeks to prevent fatigue (Frequency > 3.5x = creative burnout).
  • 6
    AOV MaximisationFree shipping above threshold (e.g., ₹999+), product bundles, upsells at checkout, cross-sell in post-purchase emails. Every ₹100 AOV improvement at 5,000 orders/month = ₹5L extra revenue with zero ad spend increase.
